

# Start Here

## Welcome

Welcome to your creative second brain: a personal collection of references, ideas, observations, and things worth returning to. It is a place to follow your curiosity, not a system you have to keep perfectly tidy.



## The philosophy

- **Collect** anything that catches your attention. A quick link or a few rough words are enough.
- **Connect** references with tags and links when you notice a real relationship.
- **Develop** a reference into a thought, research note, or project when you feel like exploring it further. Many things can simply remain references.



## Folder guide

- [[Inbox/]] — quick captures that do not have a home yet.
- [[Studios/]] — individual studios and independent creative practices. Use this for the people or teams making the work.
- [[Websites & Resources/]] — useful websites, platforms, directories, tools, archives, and publications. Use this for a resource you visit or consult, rather than the studio behind it.
- [[People/]] — individual designers, artists, and other practitioners.
- [[Topics/]] — subjects and lightweight hubs that connect notes, such as [[Topics/Creativity]], [[Topics/Bauhaus]], or [[Topics/Graphic Design]].
- [[Collections/]] — curated lists and index pages that connect related references, such as [[Collections/Competitions]] or [[Collections/Type foundries]]. Keep individual reference pages in their main folders and link to them from a collection.
- [[Projects/]] — your own experiments, ideas, and ongoing creative work.
- [[Visual References/]] — collected images, visual material, moodboards, and references that do not fit better elsewhere.
- [[Notes/]] — personal thoughts, observations, brain dumps, and developing research.
- [[How To/]] — tutorials, workflows, and practical guides.

Inside [[Websites & Resources/]], choose a subfolder by the resource’s main purpose:

- [[Websites & Resources/Design Inspiration/]] for showcases, portfolios, and work you browse for ideas.
- [[Websites & Resources/Design Archives/]] for searchable collections and reference libraries.
- [[Websites & Resources/Typography & Type/]] for type foundries, specimens, and typography resources.
- [[Websites & Resources/Branding & Identity/]] for brand-focused showcases and resources.
- [[Websites & Resources/Advertising & Campaigns/]] for campaign archives and advertising references.
- [[Websites & Resources/Film & Moving Image/]] for film libraries, moving-image collections, and director portfolios.
- [[Websites & Resources/Tools & Utilities/]] for software, generators, creative asset libraries, and practical online tools.
- [[Websites & Resources/Creative Communities/]] for collaborative platforms and creative networks.
- [[Websites & Resources/Publications & Reading/]] for magazines, articles, editorial resources, and reading material.

Choose one main location for each note. Use tags and links to show its other subjects; do not save duplicates just because something fits more than one area.



## How to capture information

- **Website or reference:** save the external link in [[Inbox/]] if you are in a hurry. When ready, give it a short note title, keep the Markdown link first, and add a few subject tags.
- **Studio or designer:** use [[Studios/]] for a practice and [[People/]] for an individual. Keep the page minimal: link, tags, and useful related notes.
- **Article:** save it under [[Websites & Resources/]] → `Publications & Reading`. Add the subject tags and a wikilink if it relates to a topic you already have.
- **Random thought:** write it in [[Notes/]] in your own words. It does not need a polished title or explanation.
- **New idea:** use [[Projects/]] if you want to develop it; use [[Notes/]] if it is only an early thought.
- **Research note:** put your own synthesis in [[Notes/]] or [[Topics/]], then link to the references that informed it.
- **Project or case study:** your own work belongs in [[Projects/]]. A project by someone else can be saved as a reference in the most relevant resource area and linked to its subjects.
- **Unclear item:** capture it in [[Inbox/]] and decide later. A rough note is better than losing the thought.

For a minimal reference, start like this:

```markdown
[Name](https://example.com)

#Typography, #ArtDirection

Related: [[Typography]]
```



## How to organise the Inbox

When you have time, look at each capture and ask: what is this mainly—a studio, person, resource, visual reference, thought, or project? Move it to that folder, add a small number of useful subject tags, and link it to related notes if a connection is clear. If you are unsure, leave it for another time. There is no required schedule and no need to empty the Inbox in one sitting.



## Tags, wikilinks, and external links

- **Tags** start with `#` and describe what a note is about, such as `#Typography` or `#ArtDirection`. Reuse the existing spelling and capitalisation. Tags help you find related material across folders; they are not a second folder system.
- **Wikilinks** use `[[Page Name]]` and connect one vault note to another. Use them when you see a meaningful relationship. Obsidian backlinks show which notes already point to the page you are reading.
- **External links** use `[Name](https://example.com)` and take you to a website. Keep them distinct from wikilinks: one points outside the vault, the other connects your notes.

## Topic hubs

Topic notes can become small hubs over time. Add a short explanation, link to the notes that genuinely relate, and connect nearby subjects. Start with what is already here, such as [[Topics/Creativity]], [[Topics/Aphantasia]], and [[Topics/Bauhaus]]. A topic page does not need to be comprehensive.



## Everyday workflows

- Find a studio, save its page in [[Studios/]], tag its disciplines, then link it to a topic when that connection matters.
- Save a site you want to revisit in [[Inbox/]], then file it under the matching [[Websites & Resources/]] subfolder.
- Gather type references in **Typography & Type** and connect selected ones to [[Typography]].
- Write a brain dump in [[Notes/]]. If a pattern appears later, link it to a topic or project.
- Start an idea in [[Projects/]] and add references as you discover them; the idea can stay rough while it develops.
- Connect a person, a movement, and a design reference when you notice a shared influence. A few useful links are enough.

## Quartz and privacy

This vault’s `content` folder is also the source for the Quartz site. The current Quartz settings ignore `.obsidian`, `private`, and `templates`; other notes in `content` may be published when the site is built and deployed. Keep sensitive material outside the published content or in a location excluded by the existing settings. When in doubt, treat a note in `content` as potentially public.
