Aphantasia is when someone can’t voluntarily picture things in their mind. They still understand what things look like and can be creative, but instead of “seeing” images, they think more in concepts, words, or structure.

For most people, imagining something like an apple comes with a faint mental image. With aphantasia, that visual just isn’t there. Some people don’t even realise this is unusual until they find out others can actually “see” things in their mind.

It can be something you’re born with or something that develops later, and it exists on a spectrum, from no imagery at all to very faint impressions. It’s estimated to affect around 2–4% of people.

It’s not considered a disorder or something that needs fixing. People with aphantasia can still be creative and successful, they just process ideas differently. In some cases, it may affect things like memory or recognising faces, but research on that is still ongoing.
