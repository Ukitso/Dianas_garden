# HELIO®

HELIO® is a wearable and digital experience designed to encourage Gen Z to reconnect with nearby nature. It brings together a UV-reactive wearable, personalised outdoor prompts and a companion app, turning small moments outside into something people can notice, track and enjoy.

The project explores how design, technology and everyday behaviour might help people build a closer relationship with the natural world, particularly in urban environments.

## Early exploration

The project began with an interest in the growing disconnect between people and nature, and the possible relationship this has with everyday wellbeing.

I was particularly interested in urban environments and the idea that nature doesn't have to mean travelling somewhere remote. It can be found in overlooked places, from moss growing between paving stones to insects, trees and small patches of sunlight.

The initial hypothesis was that making nearby nature easier to notice, and outdoor experiences feel more accessible, could encourage people to spend more time outside.

Research into [[Nature Connectedness]], [[Nature & Wellbeing]] and [[Time Outdoors]] helped explore this relationship. Looking at [[Access to Nature]] also highlighted that spending time outdoors isn't simply a matter of motivation. Location, safety, accessibility and the quality of nearby green space all play a part.

This led to a broader design question: **could a small everyday interaction help people become more aware of the nature already around them?**

## What HELIO® became

The idea developed into a wearable product and companion app, designed to make outdoor activity feel more engaging without turning it into another fitness challenge.

Rather than focusing on performance or competition, HELIO® encourages people to spend time outside at their own pace. The wearable acts as a small, physical reminder of the experience, while the app adds personalisation, tracking and opportunities to explore.

### The wearable

At the centre of HELIO® is a lightweight, UV-reactive wearable accessory.

The developed concept uses 20 reactive dots, creating a visual connection between sunlight exposure and time spent outdoors. The idea is to give users a simple way to see their outdoor activity building throughout the day.

The wearable is intended to feel closer to a piece of jewellery or a fashion accessory than a conventional fitness tracker. Its physical presence is important: something people might choose to wear as part of their everyday style.

The 20-dot system connects to the project's 20-minute outdoor prompt. Together, they create a simple visual language around spending time outside.

### The 20-minute prompt

The 20-minute concept became a central part of the product experience.

Rather than encouraging long walks or ambitious outdoor activities, HELIO® uses a manageable daily prompt to encourage people to step outside and spend time with their surroundings.

The aim isn't to prescribe how people should experience nature. It could mean sitting in the sun, taking a different route home or noticing something small on the way to work.

The 20-minute target is a design decision within the project, not an established health recommendation. Its relationship to the wider research on outdoor time is explored in [[Time Outdoors]].

### Companion app

The companion app extends the wearable experience, providing more detailed feedback and ways to engage with nearby nature.

Features explored include:

- **UV tracking:** Showing UV conditions and helping users understand their exposure.
    
- **Outdoor activity:** Tracking steps and time spent in the sun.
    
- **Personal progress:** Giving users a way to follow their activity and work towards their own goals.
    
- **Nature discovery:** Encouraging people to explore nearby outdoor spaces.
    
- **Events and community:** Highlighting opportunities to spend time outside with others.
    
- **Prompts and activities:** Offering small ideas to make outdoor experiences more engaging.
    

The app is intended to support the physical experience rather than replace it. The wearable encourages people to look up and step outside, while the digital experience gives them a reason to keep exploring.

## Brand and experience

The direction became centred on **wellness, not competition**.

HELIO® isn't intended to be another product that asks people to optimise their lives or constantly monitor their performance. It should feel encouraging, personal and enjoyable.

A few principles shaped the experience:

- **Self-paced:** No leaderboards or pressure to compete with others.
    
- **Personal:** Users can engage with the product in their own way.
    
- **Everyday:** Nature can be part of an ordinary routine, not just a destination.
    
- **Expressive:** The wearable should feel like an accessory people want to wear.
    
- **Supportive technology:** Digital features should encourage outdoor experiences rather than dominate them.
    

This also informed the relationship between the wearable and app. The physical product is the first point of interaction, with the app providing additional information and opportunities to explore.

## Creative direction

The creative exploration brought together nature, technology, fashion and urban culture.

Some of the recurring visual and cultural references included:

- **Gorp-core:** Outdoor clothing, functional accessories and the relationship between fashion and outdoor activity.
    
- **Liminality:** The spaces between urban and natural environments.
    
- **Small natural details:** Ants, moss, insects and overlooked forms of life.
    
- **Organic and artificial contrasts:** Natural textures and forms alongside technology and the built environment.
    
- **Everyday nature:** Finding visual interest in ordinary places rather than relying on idealised landscapes.
    

References included _Arrival_, _Death Stranding_, _Marathon_ and Evian, alongside locations around London.

These references helped establish a territory for the brand, combining a sense of discovery with a more contemporary, urban interpretation of nature.

## Working principles

The following principles continue to guide the project:

1. **Make nature easier to notice.** Start with the natural world that already exists in people's everyday surroundings.
    
2. **Encourage, don't pressure.** Participation should be self-directed and enjoyable.
    
3. **Keep it personal.** Allow people to choose their own goals and ways of engaging.
    
4. **Design for everyday life.** Consider short experiences and ordinary urban environments.
    
5. **Let technology support the experience.** The wearable and app should encourage interaction with the outside world rather than become the activity themselves.
    
6. **Consider access.** Account for differences in location, safety, mobility and available green space.
    
7. **Keep the experience human.** Nature contact shouldn't become another metric to optimise.
    

## Further development

Although the concept and experience have taken shape, there are still areas worth investigating if the project is developed further.

### Product and technology

- How accurately can the wearable respond to UV exposure?
    
- What are the technical and material requirements of the reactive dots?
    
- How should the wearable communicate sunlight exposure safely?
    
- What other environmental information is genuinely useful?
    

### Behaviour and experience

- Does the 20-minute prompt feel achievable and motivating?
    
- How can the product encourage continued use without relying on gamification?
    
- Can the experience encourage people to notice nature without making it feel like a task?
    

### Audience and access

- How well does the concept respond to the needs of young urban residents?
    
- How could it accommodate people with different mobility needs?
    
- How can the experience remain relevant to people without easy access to green space?
    

### Brand and communication

- How can the brand balance fashion, wellness, technology and nature?
    
- Which visual references are worth developing further?
    
- What role should community activity and collaborations play?
