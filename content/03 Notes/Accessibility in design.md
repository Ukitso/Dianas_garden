# Accessibility in Design

A lot of design doesn't prioritise accessibility at its core, leaving it fragmented or treated as something to address later.

I've been interested in why accessibility so often becomes an afterthought in the design process, rather than something considered from the beginning. Decisions around simplicity, usability, standards and inclusion all shape how people experience a product or website.

## Accessibility as a starting point

The research I've collected looks at web accessibility and some of the reasons it can be overlooked during design.

A few things I want to explore further:

- Why is accessibility so often treated as an additional requirement rather than a fundamental part of design?
    
- How do early design decisions affect the accessibility of the final experience?
    
- What role do standards and guidelines play in creating more inclusive digital experiences?
    
- Can simplicity make websites easier to use, and where does visual minimalism fall short?
    

I'm particularly interested in the gap between designing something that looks simple and designing something that is genuinely accessible. The two aren't necessarily the same.

## Further reading & references

These are some of the resources I came across while researching the subject:

- [**WCAG 2.1**](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/) – Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, outlining standards for making web content more accessible.
    
- [**In Web Design, Accessibility Shouldn't Be an Afterthought**](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/in-web-design-accessibility-shouldnt-be-an-afterthought/) – Eye on Design article exploring accessibility in the web design process.
    
- [**Motherfucking Website**](https://motherfuckingwebsite.com/) – An example of a deliberately stripped-back website, often associated with arguments around simplicity and the web.
    
- [**Brutalist Web Design**](https://brutalist-web.design/) – A collection of principles and examples exploring a more direct, stripped-back approach to web design.
