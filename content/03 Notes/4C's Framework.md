The 4Cs framework looks at a brief from four different perspectives: Consumer, Category, Company and Culture.

It's a way to explore the wider context around a brand or problem before jumping into ideas. Each area asks a different set of questions, helping to build a better understanding of the audience, the market, the brand and the world around it.

## Consumer

Understanding the people you're trying to reach.

- Do you know your audience?
    
- Do you know what they think, feel and do?
    
- What is their typical customer journey?
    
- Do you understand how they interact with the brand or product?
    

This is about getting beyond basic demographics and understanding people's behaviours, attitudes and relationships with the brand.

## Category

Understanding the market the brand operates in.

- Do you know your most obvious and less obvious competitors?
    
- Do you understand the conventions of your category?
    
- How does the category talk about the problem?
    
- What ideas or opportunities exist outside the category?
    

It's useful to look at what everyone else is doing, but also where there might be room to do something differently.

## Company

Understanding the brand itself.

- If you're creating a brand, do you have a brand purpose and vision?
    
- What are its strengths and weaknesses?
    
- If it's a product, such as an app, how could its key features make it stand out?
    
- Can anything be reframed?
    

This is about identifying what the brand has to offer and what makes it distinct.

## Culture

Understanding what's happening beyond the brand and its category.

- Do you understand current cultural trends?
    
- Can you attach your campaign or brand to any of them?
    
- Are there any social or trending topics?
    
- Can you tap into any subcultures?
    

This means looking at the wider conversations, interests and communities that could give an idea cultural relevance.

## A note to myself

The four areas give you different places to look for opportunities. An idea might come from an audience behaviour, a gap in the category, something distinctive about the company or a wider cultural shift.

It's worth looking across all four rather than focusing on the brand or audience in isolation.
