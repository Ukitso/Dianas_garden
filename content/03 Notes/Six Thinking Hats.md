# Six Thinking Hats

**Six Thinking Hats** is a structured thinking framework developed by Edward de Bono in 1985. It encourages people to approach problems, ideas and decisions from six different perspectives, helping to separate different modes of thinking rather than considering everything at once.

Each hat represents a specific way of thinking. By deliberately switching between them, individuals and teams can explore a challenge more thoroughly, identify potential issues and generate alternative solutions.

## The Six Hats

- **White Hat: Facts**  
    Focuses on objective information, data and evidence. What do we know? What information is missing?
    
- **Red Hat: Feelings**  
    Explores emotions, intuition and instinct. What is our immediate reaction?
    
- **Black Hat: Caution**  
    Identifies risks, weaknesses and potential problems. What could go wrong?
    
- **Yellow Hat: Optimism**  
    Considers benefits, opportunities and positive outcomes. What could work well?
    
- **Green Hat: Creativity**  
    Encourages new ideas, experimentation and alternative approaches. What else is possible?
    
- **Blue Hat: Process**  
    Oversees and organises the thinking process. What are we trying to achieve, and what should happen next?
    

## How It Works

The framework involves deliberately adopting each thinking perspective, either individually or as a group. Participants typically focus on one hat at a time, allowing everyone to approach the same challenge from a shared perspective.

The hats can be used in any order depending on the task and revisited throughout the process.

## Why Use It?

- Encourages more balanced and comprehensive decision-making.
    
- Helps identify assumptions, risks and opportunities.
    
- Separates emotional responses from analytical thinking.
    
- Supports collaboration by giving everyone a shared way to contribute.
    
- Encourages creative exploration beyond the most obvious solution.
    

## Application in Design

In a design context, Six Thinking Hats can be used to evaluate concepts, explore creative directions and facilitate collaborative critique. It helps designers balance creative intuition with research, practical considerations and critical evaluation before committing to a direction.

**Key takeaway:** By separating different modes of thinking, Six Thinking Hats makes it easier to explore ideas from multiple perspectives without allowing one way of thinking to dominate the process.
