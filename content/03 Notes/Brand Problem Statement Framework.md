A simple structure for turning research and strategic thinking into a clear statement of the problem, what needs to change and why it matters.

The framework follows a sequence of prompts:

## The structure

**BRAND HAS**

What is the overall ambition? What does the brand want to achieve?

**THE PROBLEM IS**

What is the main problem or tension standing in the way?

**HOWEVER**

What is happening that makes the problem more complicated? What behaviour, attitude or barrier needs to be challenged?

**WE NEED TO**

What does the brand need to do in response?

**SO THAT**

What change are we trying to create?

**HELPING TO**

What is the wider benefit or outcome?

## Putting it together

Use the prompts to build a connected statement:

- **BRAND HAS** a goal or ambition.
    
- **THE PROBLEM IS** that something is preventing it from happening.
    
- **HOWEVER** there is an underlying behaviour, attitude or tension that needs addressing.
    
- **WE NEED TO** identify what the brand should do about it.
    
- **SO THAT** a specific change can happen.
    
- **HELPING TO** create a wider positive outcome.
    

## Example from the framework

The example in the original workshop material focuses on gender inequality in gaming:

- **BRAND HAS:** The goal to stop gender inequality towards women in the community and make gaming for everyone.
    
- **THE PROBLEM IS:** There is a toxic gaming culture with an outdated mindset that women cannot play games.
    
- **HOWEVER:** People are too focused on competition and winning, and disregard their teammates, playing to win for themselves and using women as the blame when things don't go their way.
    
- **WE NEED TO:** Show that gaming isn't about gender but about teamwork and skill.
    
- **SO THAT:** The gaming community will have people working together and playing without harassing and blaming others.
    
- **HELPING TO:** Create a welcoming gaming community and decrease the need for people to hide and stay away from talking to other gamers due to fear of harassment.
    

## A note to myself

The point is to make sure the different parts connect. The problem should lead into the response, and the response should lead towards a meaningful outcome.

It's a useful way to check whether the strategy actually addresses the problem, rather than jumping straight to a campaign idea.
