# Nature Connectedness

I'm interested in how people relate to nature, not only how often they're outside. This page connects research on that relationship with the everyday experiences and conditions that shape it.

Nature connectedness can involve what people notice, understand, value and feel about the environments around them. The notes here follow those ideas through wellbeing, childhood, outdoor time, education, access, language and culture.

## A system across generations

Richardson's 2025 agent-based model links opportunity to experience nature, attention or orientation toward nature, urbanisation and intergenerational transmission. Its historical fit used nature-word frequency as proxy data; the model estimated a 61.5% decline in connectedness from 1800 to 2020 and explored future scenarios. It is a simulation informed by historical targets, not a direct survey across two centuries. The paper identifies intergenerational transmission as its strongest modelled driver and suggests that low parental connection may delay recovery even if access improves. These are model results and implications, not proof of a universal family mechanism. Related observational evidence found that parent or guardian connectedness was the strongest predictor of children's connectedness among measured child, adult and area-level characteristics; it does not establish that parents caused the difference. [1][3]

The paper proposes supporting parents and families, embedding nature engagement in school curricula, community-led stewardship, public-awareness campaigns and biodiverse, accessible urban greening. It also suggests bringing nature access into planning for education, health, transport, arts and housing, with longer-term monitoring and collaborative governance. These are policy recommendations, not evaluated programmes. [1] The 2026 publisher correction updates reference details and states that the paper's conclusions are unchanged. [2]

## Questions across the notes

- Is disconnection mainly about fewer opportunities, weaker orientation toward nature, or the interaction between them?
- Does meaningful engagement depend more on duration, frequency, attention or relationship?
- How do families and schools pass on familiarity and connection?
- Can media and community activity invite people outdoors while also addressing structural access barriers?
